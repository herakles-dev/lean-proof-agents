## Problem

Given five points in the plane, no three collinear, some four of them form the vertices of a
convex quadrilateral (the Erdős–Szekeres "Happy Ending" problem, `g(4) = 5`).

## Formal statement

```lean
theorem putnam_1962_a1
    (S : Set (ℝ × ℝ)) (hS : S.ncard = 5)
    (hnoncol : ∀ s ⊆ S, s.ncard = 3 → ¬Collinear ℝ s) :
    ∃ T ⊆ S, T.ncard = 4 ∧ ¬∃ t ∈ T, t ∈ convexHull ℝ (T \ {t})
```

## Proof

Let `E` be the extreme points of `S` (points not in the convex hull of the rest).

- **`4 ≤ E.ncard`:** any 4-subset of `E` is in convex position by subset-monotonicity of
  `convexHull` (each stays extreme in the subset). Done directly.
- **`E.ncard ≤ 3`:** let `E' := (convexHull ℝ S).extremePoints ℝ`. The **finite Krein–Milman**
  lemma (C0036, copied in as a private lemma) gives `convexHull ℝ S = convexHull ℝ E'`, so `E'`
  cannot have `≤ 2` points (else `S` lies in a segment ⇒ 3 collinear, contradicting `hnoncol`);
  with `E' ⊆ E` this forces `E'.ncard = 3`, a triangle `{X,Y,Z}`, and `S \ E' = {P,Q}`. Using the
  linear functional `L x := (Q.1−P.1)·x.2 − (Q.2−P.2)·x.1` (constant on line `PQ`), the three
  vertices are non-collinear with `PQ`, so by a sign-pigeonhole two of them, `A,B`, lie strictly on
  the same side; then `{A,B,P,Q}` is a convex quadrilateral — `A,B` are extreme in `S`, and `P,Q`
  are separated from the same-side pair by `L`.
