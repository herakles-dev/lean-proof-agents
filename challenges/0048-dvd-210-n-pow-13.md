## Problem

Show that for every integer `n`, the number `n¹³ − n` is divisible by `210`.

`210 = 2 · 3 · 5 · 7` (pairwise coprime primes), and `n¹³ ≡ n` modulo each prime `p ∈ {2,3,5,7}`
by Fermat's little theorem, since `(p − 1) ∣ 12` for each (`1,2,4,6` all divide `12`). The proof
therefore splits into **four independent modular leaves** that combine by coprimality — but here
grouped into a genuine **three-level DAG** (`{2,3,5,7} → {6,35} → 210`) to exercise the swarm's
dependency-wave scheduler at depth (the sibling [C0046](0046-thirty-divides-n-pow5-minus-n.md) was
only two levels).

Truth-gated offline (`n¹³ − n mod 210`) over the **full residue period** `n = 0…209`: `0`
counterexamples; and exponent `7` fails (`2⁷ − 2 = 126`, not divisible by `210`), confirming `13`
is meaningfully chosen (`13 − 1 = 12 = lcm(1,2,4,6)`).

## Formal statement

```lean
theorem dvd210 (n : ℤ) : (210 : ℤ) ∣ n ^ 13 - n
```

| 3 | `dvd210` | `(210 : ℤ) ∣ n¹³ - n` | `dvd6`, `dvd35` |

Wave 1 = the four prime leaves proven **in parallel** (four `lean-prover`s, each owning its own bank
file). Wave 2 = `dvd6`, `dvd35` each combine two coprime wave-1 leaves. Wave 3 = `dvd210` combines
the two composites. The Workflow dispatched each wave only once its dependencies were on the
blackboard — the async-keeper loop, now automatic.

Each modular leaf: `ZMod.intCast_zmod_eq_zero_iff_dvd (n¹³ - n) p` reduces `p ∣ n¹³ - n` to
`∀ x : ZMod p, x¹³ = x` (equivalently `x¹³ - x = 0`), closed by `decide` (finite, kernel-clean —
**no** `native_decide`). Each combiner: `IsCoprime.mul_dvd` with an explicit Bézout witness
(`2·(-1)+3·1=1`, `5·3+7·(-2)=1`, `6·6+35·(-1)=1`) + `norm_num` to rewrite the product.
